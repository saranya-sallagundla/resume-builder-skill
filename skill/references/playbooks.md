# Candidate-Type Playbooks

Pick the playbook from Step 1. Combine when needed (e.g., non-tech + domain switcher).

---

## 1. Fresher (0–1 years, no professional experience)

**The problem:** empty Work Experience section; recruiters assume "can't do anything yet."
**The fix:** prove capability, ownership, and learning velocity through real work that wasn't a job.

**Section order:** Header → Headline → Summary → Skills → Technical/Key Projects → Internships & Training → Certifications → Education → Achievements (optional)

**Headline pattern:** `<Target Title> | <Degree/Stream> | <Top 3 skills from JD>`
e.g. `Junior Data Analyst | B.Tech CSE | SQL, Python, Power BI`

**Summary (3 lines):** what they're targeting, what they've built (with one number), what they bring (one differentiator). No "seeking opportunities", no "fresher looking for".

**Projects section rules:**

- 2–4 projects, most JD-relevant first, each 2–4 XYZ bullets with real numbers (data size, users, accuracy, latency, test coverage, time saved).
- Name the stack explicitly — each is a keyword hit.
- Include one line on what broke and how they fixed it — this is the hands-on proof interviewers probe for.
- Link to GitHub/live demo if it exists (as plain URL text).
- Group project? Say "team of 3" and state *your* part — truthful and shows collaboration.

**Internships/training:** treat like jobs — company, role, dates, 2–3 bullets. Unpaid or short still counts if real.

**Education:** degree, institution, year, CGPA/percentage only if ≥ 7.5/75%. Relevant coursework as a comma list only if it adds JD keywords.

**Kill list:** hobbies, declaration lines, "objective", school details, father's name, photo (unless market requires), soft-skill lists ("hardworking, team player").

**Length:** 1 page. 2 pages only if projects and internships genuinely fill it with substance.

## 2. Domain Switcher (experienced, changing field or function)

**The problem:** recruiter sees the old domain in the title and stops reading.
**The fix:** reframe, don't hide. Old domain proves real-world professionalism; new-domain projects prove the new skill.

**Headline pattern:** `<Target Title> | <Transferable strength> | <New-domain skills>` — target title first, even if never held.
e.g. `Cloud Support Engineer | 6 yrs Production Operations & Incident Management | AWS, Linux, Terraform`

**Summary — the bridge line (mandatory):** one confident sentence connecting old to new.
"Six years running 24×7 production operations for enterprise clients; now applying the same incident, SLA, and automation discipline to cloud infrastructure, with hands-on AWS deployments and Terraform-managed environments."
Never: "looking to transition", "eager to move into", "although my background is in…"

**Transferable-skills mapping (do this explicitly before drafting):**

| Old-domain activity | New-domain language |
|---|---|
| Mainframe batch monitoring | Production support, job scheduling, SLA adherence |
| Retail store manager | P&L ownership, team leadership, customer operations |
| Teacher | Training delivery, curriculum design, stakeholder communication |
| Manual QA | Test strategy, defect lifecycle, requirement analysis |

Build a table like this for the specific candidate and use the right-hand column throughout.

**Work Experience bullets:** rewrite each old-role bullet emphasising the 20–30% that maps to the new domain. Cut what doesn't map. Keep real titles; add JD-language in parentheses if truthful: `Systems Analyst (Production Support & Incident Management)`.

**Key Projects section:** placed directly after the most recent role. New-domain hands-on work, XYZ bullets, same style as experience. This is where the switch becomes credible.

**Certifications:** new-domain certs near the top (after Skills). In-progress ones as "In Progress – Expected Mon YYYY".

**Skills section:** new-domain skills first, then transferable, then old-domain only where the JD asks.

## 3. Experienced Individual Contributor (3–12 years, same domain)

**The problem:** looks like everyone else with the same title.
**The fix:** scope + numbers + progression + 2–3 memorable specifics.

**Headline pattern:** `<Target Title> | <Years>+ Years | <Top 3 JD keywords> | <Key cert>`

**Summary:** title, years, domain, two quantified signature achievements, one specialty.

**Work Experience allocation:** current role 5–7 bullets, previous 4–5, third 3, older roles 1–2 lines each or an "Earlier Experience" one-liner. Last two roles = 60–70% of page space.

**Every role must show scope:** team size, ticket/transaction volume, systems/users supported, budget, regions, environments. Level is inferred from scope, not title.

**Progression:** numbers should grow role to role. If a title didn't change but scope did, show it in bullets ("expanded ownership from 2 to 7 applications").

**Business impact:** at least one bullet per recent role on cost, risk, compliance, revenue, or customer outcome — not only technical tasks.

## 4. Leadership / Senior (lead, manager, director, architect)

**The problem:** reads like a senior IC — all tech, no leverage.
**The fix:** strategy, people, money, risk, stakeholders.

**Headline pattern:** `<Title> | <Years>+ Years | <Domain> | <Scale signal: team size / budget / portfolio>`

**Summary:** scope of ownership (people, budget, portfolio), 2 strategic outcomes with numbers, leadership style in one phrase backed by a fact ("built and retained a 14-person team over 4 years").

**Bullet mix per recent role:** ~40% outcomes (cost, revenue, risk, uptime), ~30% people/stakeholder (hired, mentored, negotiated, presented to C-level, vendor management), ~30% technical/strategic decisions (architecture, roadmap, transformation).

**Include:** audits passed, compliance achieved, budgets owned, vendors managed, executives reported to, transformations led with before/after numbers.

**Compress:** anything hands-on from 10+ years ago to a single line. Cap stated experience at "15+ years" to avoid age filtering.

## 5. Non-Tech Roles (finance, HR, sales, marketing, operations, healthcare, education, legal, admin)

Same pipeline, different metric vocabulary. Keyword-mirror the JD exactly as for tech.

**Metrics by function:**

- **Sales/BD:** quota attainment %, revenue closed, pipeline built, deal size, win rate, accounts managed, new logos.
- **Marketing:** leads generated, CAC, conversion %, campaign ROI, audience growth, budget managed.
- **Finance/Accounting:** books closed (days), variance reduced, audit findings, budget size, savings identified, compliance (IFRS/GAAP/SOX).
- **HR/Recruiting:** hires/quarter, time-to-fill, attrition %, offer acceptance %, headcount supported, policies rolled out.
- **Operations/Supply chain:** throughput, OTIF %, cost per unit, inventory turns, SLA %, headcount led.
- **Healthcare:** patients/day, bed count, protocol compliance %, error reduction, certifications (BLS/ACLS etc.).
- **Education:** students taught, pass rate improvement, curricula designed, programs launched.
- **Legal/Compliance:** contracts reviewed, matters handled, regulatory filings, risk reduced.
- **Admin/Support:** executives supported, events managed, budget handled, process time cut.

**Tools count as keywords:** Salesforce, HubSpot, SAP, Tally, QuickBooks, Workday, Excel (advanced), Power BI, Epic, etc. — list explicitly.

**Certifications matter as much as in tech:** CPA, CFA, PMP, SHRM, CIPD, Six Sigma, Google Ads, HubSpot, nursing licences — exact names, issuing body, year.

**Persona adjustment:** think as a recruiter for that function. A CFO scanning a finance resume wants close cycle, controls, and audit outcomes in the first three bullets.

## Red-flag handling (all types)

| Red flag | Handling |
|---|---|
| Gap > 6 months | One line, factual, no apology: "Career break – caregiving / relocation / upskilling (list certs earned)". Never leave it unexplained; recruiters assume the worst. |
| Short stint (< 1 yr) | Add "(contract)" / "(project completed)" if true; or fold into a "Consulting / Contract Roles" block. |
| Title downgrade | Emphasise scope increase in bullets; use functional title in parentheses if truthful. |
| Many jobs | Group early roles under "Earlier Experience" with one line each. |
| Age signals | Drop graduation year if > 15 years ago; cap experience at "15+"; remove obsolete tech unless JD asks. |
| Overqualified | Trim leadership language, foreground hands-on bullets, align headline to the JD level. |
