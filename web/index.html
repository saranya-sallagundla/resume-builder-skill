    04 · Interview

    Fifty questions, written for your level.

    Paste the job description and your résumé. Every question below is traced to a line in one of them — no generic list. Then drill them, build the stories that answer them, and write the pitch that opens the call.

    Experience level:
    50questions ·

    Generate your 50 questions first — the deck is built from them.

    Tap, or press Space, to see what a strong answer includes

    Shaky and blank cards come back first. Ratings stay in this browser —

    One bullet from your résumé becomes one story — and one story answers several of the fifty. Fill these in your own words; they save in this browser only.

    "Tell me about yourself" — the first ninety seconds decide how the rest of the call goes. This drafts it from your résumé and the JD; edit it until it sounds like you.

    0:00

    Read it aloud at a normal pace. Under 60 seconds means it is tight enough; over 75 and they stop listening.

    The four beats

    • Headline — who you are professionally, in one line.
    • Two proof points — the strongest number you own, and the thing closest to this job.
    • Why this role — something specific from their JD, not flattery.
    • The ask — hand the conversation back deliberately.

    Questions built from the gaps and the wording in their JD. "No questions" is the most common way a good interview ends badly.

    3 · What leaves your browser
    
        
        
    
          One line at a time — never your whole résumé. Pressing an AI button sends only the single bullet you clicked, with contact details stripped. Your name, employers, dates and the rest of the document stay in this browser. That one line goes to the provider you picked under their terms, not this site's, and free tiers commonly use what you send to train their models — so treat it like a line on a public profile. Nothing is sent unless you press an AI button, and you can see the exact request first.
        
    
        
    
          
          
        
    
        
    
        
    

        
    Guardrails that are always on
    
        
      
          
    • Your pasted text is sent as data inside delimiters, with fixed instructions telling the model to ignore any instruction found inside it — a job description saying "ignore your instructions" does not get to steer it.
      • 
          
    • The reply must match an expected shape. Anything else is rejected and you keep the rule-based answer.
      • 
          
    • Model output is rendered as plain text only. It can never run code, click anything, or change your saved data.
      • 
          
    • AI never invents numbers for your résumé. It proposes wording; the figures stay yours.
      • 
          
    • Your key is kept in this browser's local storage, never in a URL, never in this repository, and never sent anywhere except the provider you chose.

The "strip contact details" option, on by default, replaces email addresses, phone numbers and links before sending; it cannot remove your name or your employers' names, so assume those are sent.
